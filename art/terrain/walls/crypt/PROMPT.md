# 壁専用原画（内蔵image_gen使用）

Use case: stylized-concept. Production pixel-art TEXTURE SOURCE SHEET for an original Japanese dungeon RPG's dedicated raised crypt walls. A perfectly square image, EXACTLY 2 columns by 2 rows of equal square panels, completely filled edge to edge, NO gutters, NO borders, NO captions. Each panel represents a DIFFERENT intentionally drawn wall surface, NOT four variations of floor. TOP LEFT: overhead wall CAP, thick worn grey stone coping blocks, broad rectangular slabs with small chips, horizontal courses, subdued moss at joints, light upper-left edges. TOP RIGHT: vertical FRONT FACE masonry, short wide stacked rectangular grey ashlar blocks, two strong horizontal courses, visible chipped bottom edges, darker than cap, hand-drawn pixel highlights on top ledges. BOTTOM LEFT: WEST/LEFT SIDE FACE of a tall stone wall, narrow elongated vertical stone courses and recessed mortar, moderately lit, vertical seams broken across courses. BOTTOM RIGHT: EAST/RIGHT SIDE FACE of wall, separate differently drawn narrower blocks, deep cooler shadows, no copy or mirroring of other panels. Strict low resolution pixel art as if each panel has 64x64 pixels, crisp square color clusters, limited grey/olive charcoal palette matching a ruined crypt, no smooth painting, no blur, no lighting glow, no characters, no props, no isometric floor diamonds. Orthographic materials for a square grid shown from slightly above and in front. These will be cropped and packed into 64px master wall-part sprites and 32px delivery sprites; keep clear readable stonework at that resolution. Panel boundaries align at exact image midpoint, no frame.

生成原案はGit対象外のart/local-originals/wall-surfaces-source.pngに保持。旧地形画像は入力も流用もしていない。4種類の専用面を別々に切り出し、原本64pxで接続端の透過輪郭・共通端画素を整え、12セルのmaster-64.pngに保存。配信32pxはbuild-wall-tileset.jsで再生成する。全エリアで共通既定の石壁として使用し、専用テーマ追加はFORMAT.mdに従う。

現行改訂：壁高0.75マス、左右側面11px幅へ強め、32px基準のatlas-32.pngを128×168px／12セルに更新した。以降は32px画像を直接編集し、build-wall-tileset.jsは寸法とハッシュ更新のみを担当。64px原本は旧版の保管用として保持し、現行画像の再生成には使用しない。画像生成の追加呼び出しは行っていない。
